# Atomic structure of Manganese

Manganese carries 25 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 25
electrons, arranged in 4 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 55, so its nucleus holds
30 neutrons alongside the 25 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 13 electrons
- shell n=4: 2 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 25 (this is what makes it Manganese and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 30 (mass number 55)
- total nucleons: about 55

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Mn's chemistry.
Manganese's chemical personality is that of a transition metal: slow oxidation — rust for iron, patina for copper, nothing visible for gold.
