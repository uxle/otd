# Thermal properties of Bromine

| quantity | °C | K |
|---|---|---|
| melting point | -7.2 | 265.95 |
| boiling point | 58.8 | 331.95 |

## What the numbers mean
Heat is vibrating atoms. In Bromine the atoms are bound by metallic, covalent,
ionic or van der Waals forces depending on its halogen nature; the melting
point tells you how strong those bonds are. With a melting point of only -7.2 °C, Bromine is among the easiest elements to melt.

Between -7.2 and 58.8 °C the element is liquid; above 58.8 °C it is a gas.
Room temperature (20 °C) sits above the
melting point, which is why Bromine is a liquid in your hand.

Typical thermal conductivity for a halogen: metals conduct heat by free
electrons (fast), nonmetals by lattice vibrations (slow). Values marked "typical"
in this library are category averages, not measured constants.
