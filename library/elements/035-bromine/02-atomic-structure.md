# Atomic structure of Bromine

Bromine carries 35 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 35
electrons, arranged in 4 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 80, so its nucleus holds
45 neutrons alongside the 35 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 18 electrons
- shell n=4: 7 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 35 (this is what makes it Bromine and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 45 (mass number 80)
- total nucleons: about 80

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Br's chemistry.
Bromine's chemical personality is that of a halogen: no direct burning; fluorine makes even glass smoke.
