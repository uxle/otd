# Thermal properties of Nihonium

| quantity | °C | K |
|---|---|---|
| melting point | 700 | 973.15 |
| boiling point | 1430 | 1703.15 |

## What the numbers mean
Heat is vibrating atoms. In Nihonium the atoms are bound by metallic, covalent,
ionic or van der Waals forces depending on its post-transition metal nature; the melting
point tells you how strong those bonds are. At 700 °C this is a mid-range melting point.

Between 700 and 1430 °C the element is liquid; above 1430 °C it is a gas.
Room temperature (20 °C) sits below the
melting point, which is why Nihonium is a solid in your hand.

Typical thermal conductivity for a post-transition metal: metals conduct heat by free
electrons (fast), nonmetals by lattice vibrations (slow). Values marked "typical"
in this library are category averages, not measured constants.
