# Atomic structure of Aluminium

Aluminium carries 13 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 13
electrons, arranged in 3 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 27, so its nucleus holds
14 neutrons alongside the 13 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 3 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 13 (this is what makes it Aluminium and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 14 (mass number 27)
- total nucleons: about 27

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Al's chemistry.
Aluminium's chemical personality is that of a post-transition metal: form protective oxide skins — aluminium's layer is 4 nanometres of armour.
