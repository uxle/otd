## One-paragraph portrait
Curium is a actinide with atomic number 96: 96 protons in the nucleus
balanced by 96 electrons in shells arranged as 2,8,18,32,25,9,2. Its standard atomic mass is
247.0 u, so one mole of Cm atoms weighs 247.0 g. The name comes from
Marie and Pierre Curie. Curium powers Mars rover instruments — it is named for the first radioactive researchers.
