# Thermal properties of Curium

| quantity | °C | K |

## What the numbers mean
Heat is vibrating atoms. In Curium the atoms are bound by metallic, covalent,
ionic or van der Waals forces depending on its actinide nature; the melting
point tells you how strong those bonds are. At 1345 °C this is a mid-range melting point.

Between 1345 and 3110 °C the element is liquid; above 3110 °C it is a gas.
Room temperature (20 °C) sits below the
melting point, which is why Curium is a solid in your hand.

Typical thermal conductivity for a actinide: metals conduct heat by free
electrons (fast), nonmetals by lattice vibrations (slow). Values marked "typical"
in this library are category averages, not measured constants.
