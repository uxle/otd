# Atomic structure of Samarium

Samarium carries 62 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 62
electrons, arranged in 6 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 150, so its nucleus holds
88 neutrons alongside the 62 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 18 electrons
- shell n=4: 24 electrons
- shell n=5: 8 electrons
- shell n=6: 2 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 62 (this is what makes it Samarium and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 88 (mass number 150)
- total nucleons: about 150

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Sm's chemistry.
Samarium's chemical personality is that of a lanthanide (rare earth): tarnish quickly in air.
