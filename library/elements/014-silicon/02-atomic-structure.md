# Atomic structure of Silicon

Silicon carries 14 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 14
electrons, arranged in 3 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 28, so its nucleus holds
14 neutrons alongside the 14 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 4 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 14 (this is what makes it Silicon and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 14 (mass number 28)
- total nucleons: about 28

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Si's chemistry.
Silicon's chemical personality is that of a metalloid: slow surface oxidation.
