# Atomic structure of Rhenium

Rhenium carries 75 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 75
electrons, arranged in 6 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 186, so its nucleus holds
111 neutrons alongside the 75 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 18 electrons
- shell n=4: 32 electrons
- shell n=5: 13 electrons
- shell n=6: 2 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 75 (this is what makes it Rhenium and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 111 (mass number 186)
- total nucleons: about 186

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Re's chemistry.
Rhenium's chemical personality is that of a transition metal: slow oxidation — rust for iron, patina for copper, nothing visible for gold.
