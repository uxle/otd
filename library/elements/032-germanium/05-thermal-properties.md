# Thermal properties of Germanium

| quantity | °C | K |
|---|---|---|
| melting point | 938.2 | 1211.35 |
| boiling point | 2833 | 3106.15 |

## What the numbers mean
Heat is vibrating atoms. In Germanium the atoms are bound by metallic, covalent,
ionic or van der Waals forces depending on its metalloid nature; the melting
point tells you how strong those bonds are. At 938.2 °C this is a mid-range melting point.

Between 938.2 and 2833 °C the element is liquid; above 2833 °C it is a gas.
Room temperature (20 °C) sits below the
melting point, which is why Germanium is a solid in your hand.

Typical thermal conductivity for a metalloid: metals conduct heat by free
electrons (fast), nonmetals by lattice vibrations (slow). Values marked "typical"
in this library are category averages, not measured constants.
