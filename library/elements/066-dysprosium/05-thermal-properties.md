## What the numbers mean
Heat is vibrating atoms. In Dysprosium the atoms are bound by metallic, covalent,
ionic or van der Waals forces depending on its lanthanide (rare earth) nature; the melting
point tells you how strong those bonds are. At 1407 °C this is a mid-range melting point.

Between 1407 and 2567 °C the element is liquid; above 2567 °C it is a gas.
Room temperature (20 °C) sits below the
melting point, which is why Dysprosium is a solid in your hand.

Typical thermal conductivity for a lanthanide (rare earth): metals conduct heat by free
electrons (fast), nonmetals by lattice vibrations (slow). Values marked "typical"
in this library are category averages, not measured constants.
