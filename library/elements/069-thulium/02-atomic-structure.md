# Atomic structure of Thulium

Thulium carries 69 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 69
electrons, arranged in 6 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 169, so its nucleus holds
100 neutrons alongside the 69 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 18 electrons
- shell n=4: 31 electrons
- shell n=5: 8 electrons
- shell n=6: 2 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 69 (this is what makes it Thulium and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 100 (mass number 169)
- total nucleons: about 169

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Tm's chemistry.
Thulium's chemical personality is that of a lanthanide (rare earth): tarnish quickly in air.
