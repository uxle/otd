# Atomic structure of Radium

Radium carries 88 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 88
electrons, arranged in 7 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 226, so its nucleus holds
138 neutrons alongside the 88 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 18 electrons
- shell n=4: 32 electrons
- shell n=5: 18 electrons
- shell n=6: 8 electrons
- shell n=7: 2 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 88 (this is what makes it Radium and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 138 (mass number 226)
- total nucleons: about 226

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Ra's chemistry.
Radium's chemical personality is that of a alkaline earth metal: oxidise steadily; Mg self-protects, then burns brilliantly once ignited.
