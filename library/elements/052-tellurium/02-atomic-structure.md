# Atomic structure of Tellurium

Tellurium carries 52 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 52
electrons, arranged in 5 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 128, so its nucleus holds
76 neutrons alongside the 52 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 18 electrons
- shell n=4: 18 electrons
- shell n=5: 6 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 52 (this is what makes it Tellurium and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 76 (mass number 128)
- total nucleons: about 128

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Te's chemistry.
Tellurium's chemical personality is that of a metalloid: slow surface oxidation.
