# Thermal properties of Tellurium

| quantity | °C | K |
|---|---|---|
| melting point | 449.5 | 722.65 |
| boiling point | 988 | 1261.15 |

## What the numbers mean
Heat is vibrating atoms. In Tellurium the atoms are bound by metallic, covalent,
ionic or van der Waals forces depending on its metalloid nature; the melting
point tells you how strong those bonds are. At 449.5 °C this is a mid-range melting point.

Between 449.5 and 988 °C the element is liquid; above 988 °C it is a gas.
Room temperature (20 °C) sits below the
melting point, which is why Tellurium is a solid in your hand.

Typical thermal conductivity for a metalloid: metals conduct heat by free
electrons (fast), nonmetals by lattice vibrations (slow). Values marked "typical"
in this library are category averages, not measured constants.
