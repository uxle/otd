# Thermal properties of Iodine

| quantity | °C | K |
|---|---|---|
| melting point | 113.7 | 386.85 |
| boiling point | 184.3 | 457.45 |

## What the numbers mean
Heat is vibrating atoms. In Iodine the atoms are bound by metallic, covalent,
ionic or van der Waals forces depending on its halogen nature; the melting
point tells you how strong those bonds are. With a melting point of only 113.7 °C, Iodine is among the easiest elements to melt.

Between 113.7 and 184.3 °C the element is liquid; above 184.3 °C it is a gas.
Room temperature (20 °C) sits below the
melting point, which is why Iodine is a solid in your hand.

Typical thermal conductivity for a halogen: metals conduct heat by free
electrons (fast), nonmetals by lattice vibrations (slow). Values marked "typical"
in this library are category averages, not measured constants.
