# Atomic structure of Iodine

Iodine carries 53 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 53
electrons, arranged in 5 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 127, so its nucleus holds
74 neutrons alongside the 53 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 18 electrons
- shell n=4: 18 electrons
- shell n=5: 7 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 53 (this is what makes it Iodine and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 74 (mass number 127)
- total nucleons: about 127

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of I's chemistry.
Iodine's chemical personality is that of a halogen: no direct burning; fluorine makes even glass smoke.
