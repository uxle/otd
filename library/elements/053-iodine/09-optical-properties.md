# Optical properties of Iodine

Library colour for Iodine: **#940094**

Metals shine because free electrons re-emit light at the surface — that is
metallic lustre. Gold and copper absorb the blue end of the spectrum and reflect
warm yellows and reds; most other metals reflect all colours equally (silver
white). Nonmetals show body colour from their electronic energy levels, and
gases glow in signature lines when excited — each element has a spectral
fingerprint used to identify it in stars, flames and street lamps.

Iodine in the flame/spectrum: a halogen emits characteristic wavelengths;
spectroscopy is how helium was found in the Sun (1868) before it was found on
Earth, and how caesium, rubidium, indium and thallium got their names from the
colours of their spectral lines.

The 3D samples in this folder render Iodine in the colour above so that
periodic-table sorting games and density experiments look consistent.
