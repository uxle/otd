# Thermal properties of Phosphorus

| quantity | °C | K |
|---|---|---|
| melting point | 44.2 | 317.35 |
| boiling point | 280.5 | 553.65 |

## What the numbers mean
Heat is vibrating atoms. In Phosphorus the atoms are bound by metallic, covalent,
ionic or van der Waals forces depending on its reactive nonmetal nature; the melting
point tells you how strong those bonds are. With a melting point of only 44.2 °C, Phosphorus is among the easiest elements to melt.

Between 44.2 and 280.5 °C the element is liquid; above 280.5 °C it is a gas.
Room temperature (20 °C) sits below the
melting point, which is why Phosphorus is a solid in your hand.

Typical thermal conductivity for a reactive nonmetal: metals conduct heat by free
electrons (fast), nonmetals by lattice vibrations (slow). Values marked "typical"
in this library are category averages, not measured constants.
