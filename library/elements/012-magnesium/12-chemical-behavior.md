# Chemical behaviour of Magnesium

Magnesium reacts like a textbook alkaline earth metal:

- with air: oxidise steadily; Mg self-protects, then burns brilliantly once ignited
- with water: moderate to vigorous — Ca/Sr/Ba react steadily, Be and Mg resist at room temperature
- with acids: vigorous — dissolves with hydrogen bubbles

Electronegativity: 1.31 on the Pauling scale — a measure of how strongly the atom pulls shared electrons.

The octet rule drives everything: atoms react to reach a full outer shell.
Metals lose electrons to become positive cations; nonmetals gain electrons to become anions; the exchange is a salt.
Magnesium follows these rules so reliably that Mendeleev used family patterns
like this to predict elements before anyone had seen them.
