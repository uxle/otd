# Atomic structure of Magnesium

Magnesium carries 12 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 12
electrons, arranged in 3 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 24, so its nucleus holds
12 neutrons alongside the 12 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 2 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 12 (this is what makes it Magnesium and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 12 (mass number 24)
- total nucleons: about 24

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Mg's chemistry.
Magnesium's chemical personality is that of a alkaline earth metal: oxidise steadily; Mg self-protects, then burns brilliantly once ignited.
