# Thermal properties of Magnesium

| quantity | °C | K |
|---|---|---|
| melting point | 650 | 923.15 |
| boiling point | 1090 | 1363.15 |

## What the numbers mean
Heat is vibrating atoms. In Magnesium the atoms are bound by metallic, covalent,
ionic or van der Waals forces depending on its alkaline earth metal nature; the melting
point tells you how strong those bonds are. At 650 °C this is a mid-range melting point.

Between 650 and 1090 °C the element is liquid; above 1090 °C it is a gas.
Room temperature (20 °C) sits below the
melting point, which is why Magnesium is a solid in your hand.

Typical thermal conductivity for a alkaline earth metal: metals conduct heat by free
electrons (fast), nonmetals by lattice vibrations (slow). Values marked "typical"
in this library are category averages, not measured constants.
