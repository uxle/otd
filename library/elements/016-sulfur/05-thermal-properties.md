# Thermal properties of Sulfur

| quantity | °C | K |
|---|---|---|
| melting point | 115.2 | 388.35 |
| boiling point | 444.6 | 717.75 |

## What the numbers mean
Heat is vibrating atoms. In Sulfur the atoms are bound by metallic, covalent,
ionic or van der Waals forces depending on its reactive nonmetal nature; the melting
point tells you how strong those bonds are. With a melting point of only 115.2 °C, Sulfur is among the easiest elements to melt.

Between 115.2 and 444.6 °C the element is liquid; above 444.6 °C it is a gas.
Room temperature (20 °C) sits below the
melting point, which is why Sulfur is a solid in your hand.

Typical thermal conductivity for a reactive nonmetal: metals conduct heat by free
electrons (fast), nonmetals by lattice vibrations (slow). Values marked "typical"
in this library are category averages, not measured constants.
