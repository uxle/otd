# Atomic structure of Sulfur

Sulfur carries 16 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 16
electrons, arranged in 3 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 32, so its nucleus holds
16 neutrons alongside the 16 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 6 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 16 (this is what makes it Sulfur and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 16 (mass number 32)
- total nucleons: about 32

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of S's chemistry.
Sulfur's chemical personality is that of a reactive nonmetal: carbon is stable; phosphorus smoulders in air (white phosphorus!).
