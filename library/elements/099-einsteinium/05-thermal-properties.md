# Thermal properties of Einsteinium

| quantity | °C | K |
|---|---|---|

| boiling point | 996 | 1269.15 |

## What the numbers mean
Heat is vibrating atoms. In Einsteinium the atoms are bound by metallic, covalent,
ionic or van der Waals forces depending on its actinide nature; the melting
point tells you how strong those bonds are. At 860 °C this is a mid-range melting point.

Between 860 and 996 °C the element is liquid; above 996 °C it is a gas.
Room temperature (20 °C) sits below the
melting point, which is why Einsteinium is a solid in your hand.

Typical thermal conductivity for a actinide: metals conduct heat by free
electrons (fast), nonmetals by lattice vibrations (slow). Values marked "typical"
in this library are category averages, not measured constants.
