# Einsteinium in air

Behaviour: **oxidise; fine uranium chips can catch fire spontaneously**

Oxidation is an electron hand-over: metal atoms give electrons to O₂. The
products — oxides — are everywhere: rust (iron oxide), patina (copper carbonate
after air + moisture), the white powder on old aluminium, and every fire you
have ever seen (rapid oxidation with light and heat).

Thermodynamics says most metals should burn; kinetics (speed) is why they do
not. Aluminium's 4 nm oxide skin forms instantly and seals the metal beneath —
passivation. Iron rusts through because its oxide is porous and flakes off.
Gold ignores oxygen entirely.

Storage rule for Einsteinium: dry and sealed; inert atmosphere for the most reactive forms.
