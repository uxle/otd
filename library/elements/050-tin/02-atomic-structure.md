# Atomic structure of Tin

Tin carries 50 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 50
electrons, arranged in 5 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 119, so its nucleus holds
69 neutrons alongside the 50 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 18 electrons
- shell n=4: 18 electrons
- shell n=5: 4 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 50 (this is what makes it Tin and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 69 (mass number 119)
- total nucleons: about 119

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Sn's chemistry.
Tin's chemical personality is that of a post-transition metal: form protective oxide skins — aluminium's layer is 4 nanometres of armour.
