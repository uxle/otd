# Atomic structure of Cadmium

Cadmium carries 48 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 48
electrons, arranged in 5 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 112, so its nucleus holds
64 neutrons alongside the 48 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 18 electrons
- shell n=4: 18 electrons
- shell n=5: 2 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 48 (this is what makes it Cadmium and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 64 (mass number 112)
- total nucleons: about 112

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Cd's chemistry.
Cadmium's chemical personality is that of a transition metal: slow oxidation — rust for iron, patina for copper, nothing visible for gold.
