# Atomic structure of Hassium

Hassium carries 108 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 108
electrons, arranged in 7 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 269, so its nucleus holds
161 neutrons alongside the 108 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 18 electrons
- shell n=4: 32 electrons
- shell n=5: 32 electrons
- shell n=6: 14 electrons
- shell n=7: 2 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 108 (this is what makes it Hassium and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 161 (mass number 269)
- total nucleons: about 269

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Hs's chemistry.
Hassium's chemical personality is that of a transition metal: slow oxidation — rust for iron, patina for copper, nothing visible for gold.
