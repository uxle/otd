# Atomic structure of Lutetium

Lutetium carries 71 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 71
electrons, arranged in 6 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 175, so its nucleus holds
104 neutrons alongside the 71 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 18 electrons
- shell n=4: 32 electrons
- shell n=5: 9 electrons
- shell n=6: 2 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 71 (this is what makes it Lutetium and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 104 (mass number 175)
- total nucleons: about 175

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Lu's chemistry.
Lutetium's chemical personality is that of a lanthanide (rare earth): tarnish quickly in air.
