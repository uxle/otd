# Atomic structure of Beryllium

Beryllium carries 4 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 4
electrons, arranged in 2 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 9, so its nucleus holds
5 neutrons alongside the 4 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 2 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 4 (this is what makes it Beryllium and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 5 (mass number 9)
- total nucleons: about 9

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Be's chemistry.
Beryllium's chemical personality is that of a alkaline earth metal: oxidise steadily; Mg self-protects, then burns brilliantly once ignited.
