# Atomic structure of Iridium

Iridium carries 77 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 77
electrons, arranged in 6 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 192, so its nucleus holds
115 neutrons alongside the 77 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 18 electrons
- shell n=4: 32 electrons
- shell n=5: 15 electrons
- shell n=6: 2 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 77 (this is what makes it Iridium and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 115 (mass number 192)
- total nucleons: about 192

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Ir's chemistry.
Iridium's chemical personality is that of a transition metal: slow oxidation — rust for iron, patina for copper, nothing visible for gold.
