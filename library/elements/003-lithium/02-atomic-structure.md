# Atomic structure of Lithium

Lithium carries 3 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 3
electrons, arranged in 2 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 7, so its nucleus holds
4 neutrons alongside the 3 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 1 electron

## The nucleus
- protons: 3 (this is what makes it Lithium and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 4 (mass number 7)
- total nucleons: about 7

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Li's chemistry.
Lithium's chemical personality is that of a alkali metal: tarnish within seconds — store under oil.
