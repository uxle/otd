# Thermal properties of Lithium

| quantity | °C | K |
|---|---|---|
| melting point | 180.5 | 453.65 |
| boiling point | 1342 | 1615.15 |

## What the numbers mean
Heat is vibrating atoms. In Lithium the atoms are bound by metallic, covalent,
ionic or van der Waals forces depending on its alkali metal nature; the melting
point tells you how strong those bonds are. With a melting point of only 180.5 °C, Lithium is among the easiest elements to melt.

Between 180.5 and 1342 °C the element is liquid; above 1342 °C it is a gas.
Room temperature (20 °C) sits below the
melting point, which is why Lithium is a solid in your hand.

Typical thermal conductivity for a alkali metal: metals conduct heat by free
electrons (fast), nonmetals by lattice vibrations (slow). Values marked "typical"
in this library are category averages, not measured constants.
