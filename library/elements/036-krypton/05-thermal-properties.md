# Thermal properties of Krypton

| quantity | °C | K |
|---|---|---|
| melting point | -157.4 | 115.75 |

## What the numbers mean
Heat is vibrating atoms. In Krypton the atoms are bound by metallic, covalent,
ionic or van der Waals forces depending on its noble gas nature; the melting
point tells you how strong those bonds are. With a melting point of only -157.4 °C, Krypton is among the easiest elements to melt.

Between -157.4 and -153.2 °C the element is liquid; above -153.2 °C it is a gas.
Room temperature (20 °C) sits above the
melting point, which is why Krypton is a gas in your hand.

Typical thermal conductivity for a noble gas: metals conduct heat by free
electrons (fast), nonmetals by lattice vibrations (slow). Values marked "typical"
in this library are category averages, not measured constants.
