# Atomic structure of Calcium

Calcium carries 20 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 20
electrons, arranged in 4 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 40, so its nucleus holds
20 neutrons alongside the 20 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 8 electrons
- shell n=4: 2 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 20 (this is what makes it Calcium and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 20 (mass number 40)
- total nucleons: about 40

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Ca's chemistry.
Calcium's chemical personality is that of a alkaline earth metal: oxidise steadily; Mg self-protects, then burns brilliantly once ignited.
