# Atomic structure of Meitnerium

Meitnerium carries 109 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 109
electrons, arranged in 7 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 278, so its nucleus holds
169 neutrons alongside the 109 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 18 electrons
- shell n=4: 32 electrons
- shell n=5: 32 electrons
- shell n=6: 15 electrons
- shell n=7: 2 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 109 (this is what makes it Meitnerium and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 169 (mass number 278)
- total nucleons: about 278

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Mt's chemistry.
Meitnerium's chemical personality is that of a transition metal: slow oxidation — rust for iron, patina for copper, nothing visible for gold.
