# Atomic structure of Ruthenium

Ruthenium carries 44 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 44
electrons, arranged in 5 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 101, so its nucleus holds
57 neutrons alongside the 44 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 18 electrons
- shell n=4: 15 electrons
- shell n=5: 1 electron

## The nucleus
- protons: 44 (this is what makes it Ruthenium and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 57 (mass number 101)
- total nucleons: about 101

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Ru's chemistry.
Ruthenium's chemical personality is that of a transition metal: slow oxidation — rust for iron, patina for copper, nothing visible for gold.
