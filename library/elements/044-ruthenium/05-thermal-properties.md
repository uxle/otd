# Thermal properties of Ruthenium

| quantity | °C | K |
|---|---|---|
| melting point | 2334 | 2607.15 |
| boiling point | 4150 | 4423.15 |

## What the numbers mean
Heat is vibrating atoms. In Ruthenium the atoms are bound by metallic, covalent,
ionic or van der Waals forces depending on its transition metal nature; the melting
point tells you how strong those bonds are. At 2334 °C this is one of the highest melting points in the entire table.

Between 2334 and 4150 °C the element is liquid; above 4150 °C it is a gas.
Room temperature (20 °C) sits below the
melting point, which is why Ruthenium is a solid in your hand.

Typical thermal conductivity for a transition metal: metals conduct heat by free
electrons (fast), nonmetals by lattice vibrations (slow). Values marked "typical"
in this library are category averages, not measured constants.
