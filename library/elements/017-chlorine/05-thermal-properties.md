# Thermal properties of Chlorine

| quantity | °C | K |
|---|---|---|
| melting point | -101.5 | 171.65 |
| boiling point | -34 | 239.15 |

## What the numbers mean
Heat is vibrating atoms. In Chlorine the atoms are bound by metallic, covalent,
ionic or van der Waals forces depending on its halogen nature; the melting
point tells you how strong those bonds are. With a melting point of only -101.5 °C, Chlorine is among the easiest elements to melt.

Between -101.5 and -34 °C the element is liquid; above -34 °C it is a gas.
Room temperature (20 °C) sits above the
melting point, which is why Chlorine is a gas in your hand.

Typical thermal conductivity for a halogen: metals conduct heat by free
electrons (fast), nonmetals by lattice vibrations (slow). Values marked "typical"
in this library are category averages, not measured constants.
