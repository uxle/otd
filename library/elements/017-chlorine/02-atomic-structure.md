# Atomic structure of Chlorine

Chlorine carries 17 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 17
electrons, arranged in 3 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 35, so its nucleus holds
18 neutrons alongside the 17 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 7 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 17 (this is what makes it Chlorine and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 18 (mass number 35)
- total nucleons: about 35

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Cl's chemistry.
Chlorine's chemical personality is that of a halogen: no direct burning; fluorine makes even glass smoke.
