# Atomic structure of Chromium

Chromium carries 24 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 24
electrons, arranged in 4 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 52, so its nucleus holds
28 neutrons alongside the 24 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 13 electrons
- shell n=4: 1 electron

## The nucleus
- protons: 24 (this is what makes it Chromium and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 28 (mass number 52)
- total nucleons: about 52

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Cr's chemistry.
Chromium's chemical personality is that of a transition metal: slow oxidation — rust for iron, patina for copper, nothing visible for gold.
