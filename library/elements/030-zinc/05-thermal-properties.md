# Thermal properties of Zinc

| quantity | °C | K |
|---|---|---|
| melting point | 419.5 | 692.65 |
| boiling point | 907 | 1180.15 |

## What the numbers mean
Heat is vibrating atoms. In Zinc the atoms are bound by metallic, covalent,
ionic or van der Waals forces depending on its transition metal nature; the melting
point tells you how strong those bonds are. At 419.5 °C this is a mid-range melting point.

Between 419.5 and 907 °C the element is liquid; above 907 °C it is a gas.
Room temperature (20 °C) sits below the
melting point, which is why Zinc is a solid in your hand.

Typical thermal conductivity for a transition metal: metals conduct heat by free
electrons (fast), nonmetals by lattice vibrations (slow). Values marked "typical"
in this library are category averages, not measured constants.
