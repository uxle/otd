# Atomic structure of Potassium

Potassium carries 19 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 19
electrons, arranged in 4 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 39, so its nucleus holds
20 neutrons alongside the 19 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 8 electrons
- shell n=4: 1 electron

## The nucleus
- protons: 19 (this is what makes it Potassium and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 20 (mass number 39)
- total nucleons: about 39

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of K's chemistry.
Potassium's chemical personality is that of a alkali metal: tarnish within seconds — store under oil.
