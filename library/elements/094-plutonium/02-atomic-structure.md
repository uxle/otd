# Atomic structure of Plutonium

Plutonium carries 94 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 94
electrons, arranged in 7 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 244, so its nucleus holds
150 neutrons alongside the 94 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 18 electrons
- shell n=4: 32 electrons
- shell n=5: 24 electrons
- shell n=6: 8 electrons
- shell n=7: 2 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 94 (this is what makes it Plutonium and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 150 (mass number 244)
- total nucleons: about 244

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Pu's chemistry.
Plutonium's chemical personality is that of a actinide: oxidise; fine uranium chips can catch fire spontaneously.
