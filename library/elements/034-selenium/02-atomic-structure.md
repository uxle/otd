# Atomic structure of Selenium

Selenium carries 34 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 34
electrons, arranged in 4 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 79, so its nucleus holds
45 neutrons alongside the 34 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 18 electrons
- shell n=4: 6 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 34 (this is what makes it Selenium and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 45 (mass number 79)
- total nucleons: about 79

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Se's chemistry.
Selenium's chemical personality is that of a reactive nonmetal: carbon is stable; phosphorus smoulders in air (white phosphorus!).
