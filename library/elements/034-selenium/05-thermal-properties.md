# Thermal properties of Selenium

| quantity | °C | K |
|---|---|---|
| melting point | 221 | 494.15 |
| boiling point | 685 | 958.15 |

## What the numbers mean
Heat is vibrating atoms. In Selenium the atoms are bound by metallic, covalent,
ionic or van der Waals forces depending on its reactive nonmetal nature; the melting
point tells you how strong those bonds are. At 221 °C this is a mid-range melting point.

Between 221 and 685 °C the element is liquid; above 685 °C it is a gas.
Room temperature (20 °C) sits below the
melting point, which is why Selenium is a solid in your hand.

Typical thermal conductivity for a reactive nonmetal: metals conduct heat by free
electrons (fast), nonmetals by lattice vibrations (slow). Values marked "typical"
in this library are category averages, not measured constants.
