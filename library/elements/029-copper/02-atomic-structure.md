# Atomic structure of Copper

Copper carries 29 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 29
electrons, arranged in 4 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 64, so its nucleus holds
35 neutrons alongside the 29 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 18 electrons
- shell n=4: 1 electron

## The nucleus
- protons: 29 (this is what makes it Copper and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 35 (mass number 64)
- total nucleons: about 64

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Cu's chemistry.
Copper's chemical personality is that of a transition metal: slow oxidation — rust for iron, patina for copper, nothing visible for gold.
