# Atomic structure of Fluorine

Fluorine carries 9 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 9
electrons, arranged in 2 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 19, so its nucleus holds
10 neutrons alongside the 9 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 7 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 9 (this is what makes it Fluorine and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 10 (mass number 19)
- total nucleons: about 19

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of F's chemistry.
Fluorine's chemical personality is that of a halogen: no direct burning; fluorine makes even glass smoke.
