# Thermal properties of Hydrogen

| quantity | °C | K |
|---|---|---|
| melting point | -259.2 | 13.95 |
| boiling point | -252.9 | 20.25 |

## What the numbers mean
Heat is vibrating atoms. In Hydrogen the atoms are bound by metallic, covalent,
ionic or van der Waals forces depending on its reactive nonmetal nature; the melting
point tells you how strong those bonds are. With a melting point of only -259.2 °C, Hydrogen is among the easiest elements to melt.

Between -259.2 and -252.9 °C the element is liquid; above -252.9 °C it is a gas.
Room temperature (20 °C) sits above the
melting point, which is why Hydrogen is a gas in your hand.

Typical thermal conductivity for a reactive nonmetal: metals conduct heat by free
electrons (fast), nonmetals by lattice vibrations (slow). Values marked "typical"
in this library are category averages, not measured constants.
