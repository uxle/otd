# Atomic structure of Silver

Silver carries 47 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 47
electrons, arranged in 5 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 108, so its nucleus holds
61 neutrons alongside the 47 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 18 electrons
- shell n=4: 18 electrons
- shell n=5: 1 electron

## The nucleus
- protons: 47 (this is what makes it Silver and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 61 (mass number 108)
- total nucleons: about 108

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Ag's chemistry.
Silver's chemical personality is that of a transition metal: slow oxidation — rust for iron, patina for copper, nothing visible for gold.
