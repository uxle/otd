# Atomic structure of Rutherfordium

Rutherfordium carries 104 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 104
electrons, arranged in 7 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 267, so its nucleus holds
163 neutrons alongside the 104 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 18 electrons
- shell n=4: 32 electrons
- shell n=5: 32 electrons
- shell n=6: 10 electrons
- shell n=7: 2 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 104 (this is what makes it Rutherfordium and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 163 (mass number 267)
- total nucleons: about 267

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Rf's chemistry.
Rutherfordium's chemical personality is that of a transition metal: slow oxidation — rust for iron, patina for copper, nothing visible for gold.
