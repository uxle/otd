# Atomic structure of Rhodium

Rhodium carries 45 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 45
electrons, arranged in 5 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 103, so its nucleus holds
58 neutrons alongside the 45 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 18 electrons
- shell n=4: 16 electrons
- shell n=5: 1 electron

## The nucleus
- protons: 45 (this is what makes it Rhodium and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 58 (mass number 103)
- total nucleons: about 103

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Rh's chemistry.
Rhodium's chemical personality is that of a transition metal: slow oxidation — rust for iron, patina for copper, nothing visible for gold.
