# Atomic structure of Barium

Barium carries 56 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 56
electrons, arranged in 6 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 137, so its nucleus holds
81 neutrons alongside the 56 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 18 electrons
- shell n=4: 18 electrons
- shell n=5: 8 electrons
- shell n=6: 2 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 56 (this is what makes it Barium and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 81 (mass number 137)
- total nucleons: about 137

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Ba's chemistry.
Barium's chemical personality is that of a alkaline earth metal: oxidise steadily; Mg self-protects, then burns brilliantly once ignited.
