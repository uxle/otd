# Chemical behaviour of Moscovium

Moscovium reacts like a textbook post-transition metal:

- with air: form protective oxide skins — aluminium's layer is 4 nanometres of armour
- with water: slow — aluminium protects itself with an oxide skin, lead grows a passive layer
- with acids: dissolve unless the oxide skin protects them (aluminium resists until the skin breaks)

Electronegativity: conventionally not quoted for this element.

The octet rule drives everything: atoms react to reach a full outer shell.
Metals lose electrons to become positive cations; nonmetals gain electrons to become anions; the exchange is a salt.
Moscovium follows these rules so reliably that Mendeleev used family patterns
like this to predict elements before anyone had seen them.
