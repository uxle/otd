## One-paragraph portrait
Californium is a actinide with atomic number 98: 98 protons in the nucleus
balanced by 98 electrons in shells arranged as 2,8,18,32,28,8,2. Its standard atomic mass is
251.0 u, so one mole of Cf atoms weighs 251.0 g. The name comes from
California. One microgram of californium fires 170 million neutrons per minute — a start button for reactors.
