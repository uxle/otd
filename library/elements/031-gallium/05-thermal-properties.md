# Thermal properties of Gallium

| quantity | °C | K |
|---|---|---|
| melting point | 29.8 | 302.95 |
| boiling point | 2400 | 2673.15 |

## What the numbers mean
Heat is vibrating atoms. In Gallium the atoms are bound by metallic, covalent,
ionic or van der Waals forces depending on its post-transition metal nature; the melting
point tells you how strong those bonds are. With a melting point of only 29.8 °C, Gallium is among the easiest elements to melt.

Between 29.8 and 2400 °C the element is liquid; above 2400 °C it is a gas.
Room temperature (20 °C) sits below the
melting point, which is why Gallium is a solid in your hand.

Typical thermal conductivity for a post-transition metal: metals conduct heat by free
electrons (fast), nonmetals by lattice vibrations (slow). Values marked "typical"
in this library are category averages, not measured constants.
