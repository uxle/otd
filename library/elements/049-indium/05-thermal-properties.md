# Thermal properties of Indium

| quantity | °C | K |
|---|---|---|
| melting point | 156.6 | 429.75 |
| boiling point | 2072 | 2345.15 |

## What the numbers mean
Heat is vibrating atoms. In Indium the atoms are bound by metallic, covalent,
ionic or van der Waals forces depending on its post-transition metal nature; the melting
point tells you how strong those bonds are. With a melting point of only 156.6 °C, Indium is among the easiest elements to melt.

Between 156.6 and 2072 °C the element is liquid; above 2072 °C it is a gas.
Room temperature (20 °C) sits below the
melting point, which is why Indium is a solid in your hand.

Typical thermal conductivity for a post-transition metal: metals conduct heat by free
electrons (fast), nonmetals by lattice vibrations (slow). Values marked "typical"
in this library are category averages, not measured constants.
