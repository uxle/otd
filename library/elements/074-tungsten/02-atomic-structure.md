# Atomic structure of Tungsten

Tungsten carries 74 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 74
electrons, arranged in 6 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 184, so its nucleus holds
110 neutrons alongside the 74 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 18 electrons
- shell n=4: 32 electrons
- shell n=5: 12 electrons
- shell n=6: 2 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 74 (this is what makes it Tungsten and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 110 (mass number 184)
- total nucleons: about 184

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of W's chemistry.
Tungsten's chemical personality is that of a transition metal: slow oxidation — rust for iron, patina for copper, nothing visible for gold.
