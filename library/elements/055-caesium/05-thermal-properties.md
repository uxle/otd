# Thermal properties of Caesium

| quantity | °C | K |
|---|---|---|
| melting point | 28.4 | 301.55 |
| boiling point | 671 | 944.15 |

## What the numbers mean
Heat is vibrating atoms. In Caesium the atoms are bound by metallic, covalent,
ionic or van der Waals forces depending on its alkali metal nature; the melting
point tells you how strong those bonds are. With a melting point of only 28.4 °C, Caesium is among the easiest elements to melt.

Between 28.4 and 671 °C the element is liquid; above 671 °C it is a gas.
Room temperature (20 °C) sits below the
melting point, which is why Caesium is a solid in your hand.

Typical thermal conductivity for a alkali metal: metals conduct heat by free
electrons (fast), nonmetals by lattice vibrations (slow). Values marked "typical"
in this library are category averages, not measured constants.
