# Atomic structure of Oxygen

Oxygen carries 8 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 8
electrons, arranged in 2 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 16, so its nucleus holds
8 neutrons alongside the 8 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 6 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 8 (this is what makes it Oxygen and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 8 (mass number 16)
- total nucleons: about 16

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of O's chemistry.
Oxygen's chemical personality is that of a reactive nonmetal: carbon is stable; phosphorus smoulders in air (white phosphorus!).
