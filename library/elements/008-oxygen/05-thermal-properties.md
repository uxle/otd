# Thermal properties of Oxygen

| quantity | °C | K |
|---|---|---|
| melting point | -218.8 | 54.35 |
| boiling point | -183 | 90.15 |

## What the numbers mean
Heat is vibrating atoms. In Oxygen the atoms are bound by metallic, covalent,
ionic or van der Waals forces depending on its reactive nonmetal nature; the melting
point tells you how strong those bonds are. With a melting point of only -218.8 °C, Oxygen is among the easiest elements to melt.

Between -218.8 and -183 °C the element is liquid; above -183 °C it is a gas.
Room temperature (20 °C) sits above the
melting point, which is why Oxygen is a gas in your hand.

Typical thermal conductivity for a reactive nonmetal: metals conduct heat by free
electrons (fast), nonmetals by lattice vibrations (slow). Values marked "typical"
in this library are category averages, not measured constants.
