# Atomic structure of Cobalt

Cobalt carries 27 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 27
electrons, arranged in 4 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 59, so its nucleus holds
32 neutrons alongside the 27 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 15 electrons
- shell n=4: 2 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 27 (this is what makes it Cobalt and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 32 (mass number 59)
- total nucleons: about 59

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Co's chemistry.
Cobalt's chemical personality is that of a transition metal: slow oxidation — rust for iron, patina for copper, nothing visible for gold.
