# Atomic structure of Boron

Boron carries 5 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 5
electrons, arranged in 2 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 11, so its nucleus holds
6 neutrons alongside the 5 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 3 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 5 (this is what makes it Boron and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 6 (mass number 11)
- total nucleons: about 11

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of B's chemistry.
Boron's chemical personality is that of a metalloid: slow surface oxidation.
