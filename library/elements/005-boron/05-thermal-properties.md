# Thermal properties of Boron

| quantity | °C | K |
|---|---|---|
| melting point | 2076 | 2349.15 |
| boiling point | 3927 | 4200.15 |

## What the numbers mean
Heat is vibrating atoms. In Boron the atoms are bound by metallic, covalent,
ionic or van der Waals forces depending on its metalloid nature; the melting
point tells you how strong those bonds are. At 2076 °C this is one of the highest melting points in the entire table.

Between 2076 and 3927 °C the element is liquid; above 3927 °C it is a gas.
Room temperature (20 °C) sits below the
melting point, which is why Boron is a solid in your hand.

Typical thermal conductivity for a metalloid: metals conduct heat by free
electrons (fast), nonmetals by lattice vibrations (slow). Values marked "typical"
in this library are category averages, not measured constants.
