# Atomic structure of Vanadium

Vanadium carries 23 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 23
electrons, arranged in 4 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 51, so its nucleus holds
28 neutrons alongside the 23 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 11 electrons
- shell n=4: 2 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 23 (this is what makes it Vanadium and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 28 (mass number 51)
- total nucleons: about 51

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of V's chemistry.
Vanadium's chemical personality is that of a transition metal: slow oxidation — rust for iron, patina for copper, nothing visible for gold.
