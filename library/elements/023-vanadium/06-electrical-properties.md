# Electrical properties of Vanadium

Classification: **conductor — free electrons carry current**

Metals conduct because their outer electrons detach into a shared "sea" and drift
when a voltage is applied. Nonmetals keep their electrons locked in bonds.
Metalloids sit in between — silicon's conductivity can be tuned by doping, which
is the entire foundation of the computer industry.

A 1 cm³ cube of Vanadium in this library is drawn with its true density 6.11 g/cm³; electrical values are classed rather than tabulated for elements without exact engine materials.

Ohm's law reminder: V = I·R. Conductivity σ is the inverse of resistivity ρ.
Superconductivity, where resistance drops to exactly zero, appears in many
metals and alloys below their critical temperature.
