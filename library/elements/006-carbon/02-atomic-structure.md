# Atomic structure of Carbon

Carbon carries 6 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 6
electrons, arranged in 2 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 12, so its nucleus holds
6 neutrons alongside the 6 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 4 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 6 (this is what makes it Carbon and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 6 (mass number 12)
- total nucleons: about 12

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of C's chemistry.
Carbon's chemical personality is that of a reactive nonmetal: carbon is stable; phosphorus smoulders in air (white phosphorus!).
