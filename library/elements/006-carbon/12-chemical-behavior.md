# Chemical behaviour of Carbon

Carbon reacts like a textbook reactive nonmetal:

- with air: carbon is stable; phosphorus smoulders in air (white phosphorus!)
- with water: varies — hydrogen burns, oxygen dissolves into water (that is what water is), carbon is inert
- with acids: no metal-style reaction — carbon resists, phosphorus and sulfur oxidise instead

Electronegativity: 2.55 on the Pauling scale — a measure of how strongly the atom pulls shared electrons.

The octet rule drives everything: atoms react to reach a full outer shell.
Metals lose electrons to become positive cations; nonmetals gain electrons to become anions; the exchange is a salt.
Carbon follows these rules so reliably that Mendeleev used family patterns
like this to predict elements before anyone had seen them.
