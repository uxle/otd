# Atomic structure of Cerium

Cerium carries 58 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 58
electrons, arranged in 6 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 140, so its nucleus holds
82 neutrons alongside the 58 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 18 electrons
- shell n=4: 19 electrons
- shell n=5: 9 electrons
- shell n=6: 2 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 58 (this is what makes it Cerium and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 82 (mass number 140)
- total nucleons: about 140

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Ce's chemistry.
Cerium's chemical personality is that of a lanthanide (rare earth): tarnish quickly in air.
