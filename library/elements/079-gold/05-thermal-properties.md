# Thermal properties of Gold

| quantity | °C | K |
|---|---|---|
| melting point | 1064.2 | 1337.35 |

## What the numbers mean
Heat is vibrating atoms. In Gold the atoms are bound by metallic, covalent,
ionic or van der Waals forces depending on its transition metal nature; the melting
point tells you how strong those bonds are. At 1064.2 °C this is a mid-range melting point.

Between 1064.2 and 2856 °C the element is liquid; above 2856 °C it is a gas.
Room temperature (20 °C) sits below the
melting point, which is why Gold is a solid in your hand.

Typical thermal conductivity for a transition metal: metals conduct heat by free
electrons (fast), nonmetals by lattice vibrations (slow). Values marked "typical"
in this library are category averages, not measured constants.
