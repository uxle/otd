# Atomic structure of Thallium

Thallium carries 81 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 81
electrons, arranged in 6 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 204, so its nucleus holds
123 neutrons alongside the 81 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 18 electrons
- shell n=4: 32 electrons
- shell n=5: 18 electrons
- shell n=6: 3 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 81 (this is what makes it Thallium and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 123 (mass number 204)
- total nucleons: about 204

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Tl's chemistry.
Thallium's chemical personality is that of a post-transition metal: form protective oxide skins — aluminium's layer is 4 nanometres of armour.
