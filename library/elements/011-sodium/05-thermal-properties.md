## What the numbers mean
Heat is vibrating atoms. In Sodium the atoms are bound by metallic, covalent,
ionic or van der Waals forces depending on its alkali metal nature; the melting
point tells you how strong those bonds are. With a melting point of only 97.8 °C, Sodium is among the easiest elements to melt.

Between 97.8 and 883 °C the element is liquid; above 883 °C it is a gas.
Room temperature (20 °C) sits below the
melting point, which is why Sodium is a solid in your hand.

Typical thermal conductivity for a alkali metal: metals conduct heat by free
electrons (fast), nonmetals by lattice vibrations (slow). Values marked "typical"
in this library are category averages, not measured constants.
