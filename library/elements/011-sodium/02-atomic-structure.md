# Atomic structure of Sodium

Sodium carries 11 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 11
electrons, arranged in 3 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 23, so its nucleus holds
12 neutrons alongside the 11 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 1 electron

## The nucleus
- protons: 11 (this is what makes it Sodium and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 12 (mass number 23)
- total nucleons: about 23

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Na's chemistry.
Sodium's chemical personality is that of a alkali metal: tarnish within seconds — store under oil.
