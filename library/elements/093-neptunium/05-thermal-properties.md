## What the numbers mean
Heat is vibrating atoms. In Neptunium the atoms are bound by metallic, covalent,
ionic or van der Waals forces depending on its actinide nature; the melting
point tells you how strong those bonds are. At 644 °C this is a mid-range melting point.

Between 644 and 3902 °C the element is liquid; above 3902 °C it is a gas.
Room temperature (20 °C) sits below the
melting point, which is why Neptunium is a solid in your hand.

Typical thermal conductivity for a actinide: metals conduct heat by free
electrons (fast), nonmetals by lattice vibrations (slow). Values marked "typical"
in this library are category averages, not measured constants.
