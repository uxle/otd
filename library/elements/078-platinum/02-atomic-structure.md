# Atomic structure of Platinum

Platinum carries 78 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 78
electrons, arranged in 6 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 195, so its nucleus holds
117 neutrons alongside the 78 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 18 electrons
- shell n=4: 32 electrons
- shell n=5: 16 electrons
- shell n=6: 1 electron

## The nucleus
- protons: 78 (this is what makes it Platinum and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 117 (mass number 195)
- total nucleons: about 195

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Pt's chemistry.
Platinum's chemical personality is that of a transition metal: slow oxidation — rust for iron, patina for copper, nothing visible for gold.
