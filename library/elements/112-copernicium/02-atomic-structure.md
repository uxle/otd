# Atomic structure of Copernicium

Copernicium carries 112 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 112
electrons, arranged in 7 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 285, so its nucleus holds
173 neutrons alongside the 112 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 18 electrons
- shell n=4: 32 electrons
- shell n=5: 32 electrons
- shell n=6: 18 electrons
- shell n=7: 2 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 112 (this is what makes it Copernicium and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 173 (mass number 285)
- total nucleons: about 285

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Cn's chemistry.
Copernicium's chemical personality is that of a transition metal: slow oxidation — rust for iron, patina for copper, nothing visible for gold.
