# Thermal properties of Xenon

| quantity | °C | K |
|---|---|---|
| melting point | -111.7 | 161.45 |
| boiling point | -108 | 165.15 |

## What the numbers mean
Heat is vibrating atoms. In Xenon the atoms are bound by metallic, covalent,
ionic or van der Waals forces depending on its noble gas nature; the melting
point tells you how strong those bonds are. With a melting point of only -111.7 °C, Xenon is among the easiest elements to melt.

Between -111.7 and -108 °C the element is liquid; above -108 °C it is a gas.
Room temperature (20 °C) sits above the
melting point, which is why Xenon is a gas in your hand.

Typical thermal conductivity for a noble gas: metals conduct heat by free
electrons (fast), nonmetals by lattice vibrations (slow). Values marked "typical"
in this library are category averages, not measured constants.
