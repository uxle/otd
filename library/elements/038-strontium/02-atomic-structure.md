# Atomic structure of Strontium

Strontium carries 38 protons. A neutral atom therefore holds exactly 38
electrons, arranged in 5 shells around the
nucleus. The most common isotope has mass number 88, so its nucleus holds
50 neutrons alongside the 38 protons.

## Electron shells
- shell n=1: 2 electrons
- shell n=2: 8 electrons
- shell n=3: 18 electrons
- shell n=4: 8 electrons
- shell n=5: 2 electrons

## The nucleus
- protons: 38 (this is what makes it Strontium and nothing else)
- neutrons: about 50 (mass number 88)
- total nucleons: about 88

Electrons fill shells from the inside out: 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second,
18 in the third (with sub-shell bookkeeping the pattern continues 8, 18, 32 …).
The outermost electrons — the valence electrons — decide all of Sr's chemistry.
Strontium's chemical personality is that of a alkaline earth metal: oxidise steadily; Mg self-protects, then burns brilliantly once ignited.
