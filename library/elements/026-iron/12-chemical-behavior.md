# Chemical behaviour of Iron

Iron reacts like a textbook transition metal:

- with air: slow oxidation — rust for iron, patina for copper, nothing visible for gold
- with water: mostly slow or none at room temperature — iron rusts over years, gold and platinum ignore water
- with acids: most dissolve (iron + acid = hydrogen + salt); noble metals Cu/Ag/Au/Pt resist

Electronegativity: 1.83 on the Pauling scale — a measure of how strongly the atom pulls shared electrons.

The octet rule drives everything: atoms react to reach a full outer shell.
Metals lose electrons to become positive cations; nonmetals gain electrons to become anions; the exchange is a salt.
Iron follows these rules so reliably that Mendeleev used family patterns
like this to predict elements before anyone had seen them.
