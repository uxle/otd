# Thermal properties of Mercury

| quantity | °C | K |
|---|---|---|
| melting point | -38.8 | 234.35 |
| boiling point | 356.7 | 629.85 |

## What the numbers mean
Heat is vibrating atoms. In Mercury the atoms are bound by metallic, covalent,
ionic or van der Waals forces depending on its transition metal nature; the melting
point tells you how strong those bonds are. With a melting point of only -38.8 °C, Mercury is among the easiest elements to melt.

Between -38.8 and 356.7 °C the element is liquid; above 356.7 °C it is a gas.
Room temperature (20 °C) sits above the
melting point, which is why Mercury is a liquid in your hand.

Typical thermal conductivity for a transition metal: metals conduct heat by free
electrons (fast), nonmetals by lattice vibrations (slow). Values marked "typical"
in this library are category averages, not measured constants.
