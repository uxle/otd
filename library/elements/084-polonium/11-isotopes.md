# Isotopes of Polonium

Isotopes have the same proton count (Z = 84) but different neutron counts,
so they share chemistry but differ in mass and nuclear stability.

- common isotope: Po-209 (84p + 125n)
- stability: no stable isotopes — every isotope decays; the table lists the most long-lived
- decay modes: alpha (helium nucleus) and beta (electron) emission chains down toward lead

Radioactive decay follows the exponential law N(t) = N₀·e^(−λt); the half-life
t½ = ln 2 / λ is the time half the atoms decay. Half-lives range from fractions
of a second to longer than the age of the universe — which is why some natural
radioactivity (uranium, thorium, potassium-40) is still with us.
